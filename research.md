# Competitive Research: Consumer Agent-Referral Platforms (as of 2026-11-17)

Scope: read-only web research. Many review sources are competitors writing about each other (Clever, HomeLight, Real Estate Witch, Houwzer, Own Luxury Homes). Their claims are marked as such. **[UNVERIFIED]** means I could not confirm it from a primary source.

---

## 1. Competitor profiles

### RamseyTrusted (ramseysolutions.com/real-estate)
- **Value prop:** "Agents who share our values." Free; agents are "thoroughly vetted to earn our stamp of approval." Agent-recruiting headline: "Leads are just a list. Referrals are a relationship."
- **Matching:** Consumer request goes to **up to 3 local pros**, who then "reach out to win the business." Consumer can also browse and message agents directly. No published speed SLA.
- **Vetting claimed:** minimum 50 career closings, 2+ years full-time, "top 10% of their market"; agents get a Ramsey coach. Leads are phone-verified and give timeline and down payment. (Ramsey pages via search; help-center page returned 403.)
- **Consumer gets:** brand trust and debt-free/values framing. No rebate or discount. Standard commission applies.
- **Agent fee:** not published. Inman (2021) quotes an agent assuming "30 percent"; a former ELP agent reported a $350 monthly fee on top. **[UNVERIFIED current %]**
- **Weaknesses:** small network (~2,500 agents per one competitor source); Clever says vetting is "not always rigorous," with complaints about communication and local expertise; the fee is not disclosed to consumers. Inman covered an eXp/Ramsey dispute about referral transparency (2021).
- Sources: https://www.ramseysolutions.com/real-estate/real-estate-pro-referrals · https://listwithclever.com/reviews/dave-ramsey-elp-realtors/ · https://www.inman.com/2021/06/25/exp-dave-ramsey-conflict-ignites-debate-about-referral-programs/

### HomeLight (homelight.com)
- **Value prop:** find a top agent "in minutes"; analyzes "27 million transactions." Claims "top 5% of agents sell for as much as 10% more" (self-reported).
- **Matching:** algorithmic quiz (home type, year built, timeline / buyer preferences), then a short list. Reviewers report 1 to 3 agents, often calling "within minutes." Says agents "cannot pay to be listed," **but only agents who signed the referral agreement are matched**.
- **Vetting:** "performance and professionalism." Criteria not published; Clever says "top 1% by sales volume."
- **Consumer extras:** Simple Sale (investor cash offer in 24 hours, close in about 7 days), Buy Before You Sell (2.4% fee, 47 states), Trade-In, mortgage.
- **Agent fee:** **33%** of gross commission since Oct 2022 (was 25%). The tail is 3 years from the date HomeLight hands over the contact.
- **Trust:** BBB A+ (accredited 2019), about 4.5 stars on Google.
- **Weaknesses:** the most common complaint is a "flurry of calls and texts" after a home-value or cash-offer request, with contact info sent to several agents (BBB complaint quoted on SmartCustomer). One Trustpilot reviewer reports "200 calls." Agents call the 3-year tail a "blanket kickback" and say referral clients get less effort.
- Sources: https://www.homelight.com/blog/homelight-reviews · https://inman.com/2022/10/25/homelight-raises-real-estate-agent-referral-fee-to-33 · https://help.homelight.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002947414-what-does-homelight-cost- · https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/homelight.com?page=7

### Clever Real Estate (listwithclever.com)
- **Value prop:** sell for a **1.5% listing fee ($3,000 min)** with a full-service agent from a major brokerage. Buyers get **Clever Cash Back** of $250, or $500 if they buy and sell (not available in about 9 states).
- **Matching:** human concierge (licensed) calls **within ~5 minutes**. You can interview as many agents as you like or walk away. Claims 14,000+ agents.
- **Agent fee:** referral fee at close (percentage not published on consumer pages). Agents accept the discounted rate.
- **Trust:** about 4,000+ Trustpilot reviews at roughly 4.9 (aggregator figure, not live-checked). Heavy SEO content and review-site empire.
- **Weaknesses:** agents charging more than the promised 1.5%; first match not a fit; agent communication. The $3k minimum hurts low-price homes. Buyer agent fee is still separate and negotiable. The concierge call feels like sales to some users.
- Sources: https://www.listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/clever-real-estate-reviews/ · https://www.bankrate.com/real-estate/clever-review · https://listwithclever.com/faq

### Zillow (Premier Agent → Zillow Preferred, formerly Flex)
- **Value prop:** "Contact agent" / "Request a tour" buttons on every listing. The consumer often thinks they are reaching the listing agent or a Zillow employee (ConsumerAffairs reviewer).
- **Matching:** "performance-based routing" by ZIP to Preferred partner agents. Per Inman (Nov 2026), about two-thirds of buyer connections already go to Preferred, and Zillow says Preferred will handle **all** buyer connections by early 2027. Entry is invitation-only. Agents must use Follow Up Boss with calling.
- **Agent fee:** success fee at close, **up to 40%** (lawsuit and press figure). Owed on the first 2 transactions within 2 years of the lead.
- **Consumer gets:** fast tours. Nothing financial.
- **Weaknesses / legal:** Sept 2025 class action (Hagens Berman, Cohen Milstein) says the undisclosed fee inflates commissions and misleads consumers. Taylor v. Zillow says the "Touring Agreement" calls services "free" when commission is still owed. Nov 2025 and Jan 2026 suits allege agents are pressured to steer buyers to Zillow Home Loans (RESPA). Zillow's own 2017 survey: consumers "never heard back" from Premier Agents 49% of the time. Agents call leads "garbage" and report $800/month for 4 leads (old PA model). Zillow says fees are B2B and claims no statistically significant commission difference (2025 internal study).
- Sources: https://www.zillow.com/preferred/compliance/ · https://www.zillow.com/z/flex-performance-terms/performance-standards · https://news.inman.com/2025/09/19/zillow-sued-over-allegedly-deceptive-flex-agent-tactics/ · https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/01/20/zillow-hit-with-another-lawsuit-over-allegations-of-steering · https://www.classaction.org/media/taylor-v-zillow-inc-et-al-complaint.pdf · https://www.inman.com/2018/11/13/are-zillows-premier-agent-changes-working-for-agents/

### Redfin Partner Agents (now part of Rocket, acquired July 2025)
- **Value prop:** Redfin's brand and its 1–1.5% listing fee (market-dependent), but **Partner Agents don't get Redfin pricing or incentives** ("do not qualify for Redfin's listing fees or other incentives"). Partners are used when Redfin agents are busy, the area is out of market, or the price is under Redfin's minimum.
- **Matching:** Redfin assigns the partner. Partners must meet the Partner Program Performance Standards (response, conversion, survey).
- **Agent fee:** historically **30%** (Redfin's own news post); third parties say 30–35%. Flat-fee schedules have applied to some connections since Aug 2024. Fee is due within 15 days of close.
- **Consumer gets:** Sign & Save rebate only with Redfin employee agents. **Rocket Preferred Pricing:** a 1-point rate cut for year 1 (up to $6,000) if you use a Redfin agent and Rocket financing.
- **Weaknesses:** a two-tier experience (the consumer may not know they got a partner, not a W-2 Redfin agent); pressure to bundle the mortgage.
- Sources: https://partneragents.redfin.com/hc/en-us/articles/10644751126541-Overview-Partner-Program-Performance-Standards · https://partneragents.redfin.com/hc/en-us/articles/42713472498189-Module-6-Paying-a-Referral-Fee · https://www.redfin.com/news/redfin_creates_a_marketplace_for_agents/ · https://www.housingwire.com/articles/rocket-revamps-real-estate-arm-moves-home-search-to-redfin/

### Rocket Homes
- **Value prop:** cash back ("up to $750" in an undated flyer **[UNVERIFIED current]**), plus mortgage tie-in. Home search was moved to Redfin in Aug 2025, and the 12,000-partner network is being consolidated with Redfin's.
- **Agent fee:** **35%** of the brokerage commission (CFPB complaint, Dec 2024).
- **Weaknesses / legal:** the CFPB sued Rocket Homes and Jason Mitchell Group in Dec 2024 over an alleged kickback scheme in which agents were pressured to steer clients to Rocket Mortgage. A Jan 2026 RESPA class action makes similar claims. All are allegations; I did not find outcomes.
- Sources: https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_ea-rocket-respa-complaint_2024-12.pdf · https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/01/26/rocket-hit-with-respa-class-action-lawsuit

### UpNest (owned by Realtor.com/Move since 2017)
- **Value prop:** agents **compete for you**. You get **3–5 sealed proposals within 24 hours**, showing commission, experience and services.
- **Agent fee:** about **30%** at close (25–35% across sources).
- **Weaknesses:** savings are not guaranteed (typical proposals are 2–2.5% listing). A 2025 comparison found savings "comparable to what you might find independently." Competition on price is not competition on quality. Houwzer says the standalone agent app was retired in May 2026 and the service rebranded **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- Sources: https://bankrate.com/real-estate/upnest-review · https://houwzer.com/blog/upnest-reviews/ · https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/whats-the-upnest-referral-fee-5-things-to-know/

### Realtor.com (Market VIP, Connections Plus, ReadyConnect Concierge, formerly Opcity)
- **Model:** Connections Plus is a **shared** lead sold to agents by ZIP; one competitor claims the same lead goes to 2–4 agents **[UNVERIFIED]**. Market VIP is marketed as exclusive per ZIP. ReadyConnect Concierge is pay-at-close: historically 30% (homes ≤$150k) or 35% (above), with 28–40% cited in guides.
- **Weaknesses:** a BBB complaint reports being "contacted by multiple agents at the same time" despite exclusivity; "every shared lead is a race," so speed beats fit. Concierge transfers live calls to whichever agent answers.
- Sources: https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/realtor-com-leads-reviews/ · https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-opcitys-referral-fee/

### FastExpert
- **Value prop:** "compare top agents" through profiles, video intros (about 5,000) and reviews. Claims 95,000 agent members, 2M+ aggregated reviews and 8M sales records. Bootstrapped; California broker license #01950016.
- **Matching:** consumer browses ranked profiles or submits a request. Ranking is driven by reviews. Agents can buy a premium membership.
- **Agent fee:** likely about 25% at close (third-party estimate) **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Weaknesses:** almost no independent reviews; reviews are agent-solicited and self-selected; review counts are inconsistent (1M vs 2M); paid premium tiers muddy "ranking."
- Sources: https://www.fastexpert.com/fact-sheet/ · https://houwzer.com/?p=2886

### OJO / Movoto
- **Model:** Movoto portal plus OJO Select / Pro+ networks. Movoto Advantage charges agents a **monthly fee plus a 35% success fee** (Inman 2026). Historically 35–45%.
- **Trust:** Trustpilot about 4.5 (621 reviews); BBB A+ per Movoto.
- **Weaknesses:** Trustpilot themes are bait-and-switch, rude or pushy agents, trouble removing listings, and unsubscribe not working. Agents complain about uneven lead distribution and leads that need "nurturing."
- Sources: https://www.trustpilot.com/review/movoto.com?page=3 · https://kimola.com/reports/essential-movoto-real-estate-customer-insights-report-trustpilot-en-us-146747 · https://agenthelp.movoto.com/select-network/receiving-and-winning-more-leads

## 2. Cross-cutting consumer pain points (evidence)
1. **Data sprayed to many agents, and call floods** (HomeLight BBB/Trustpilot, Realtor.com shared leads, Movoto unsubscribe failures).
2. **Pay-to-play ranking:** only fee-signing agents are matched (HomeLight), ZIPs are bought (Realtor.com), premium tiers are sold (FastExpert), routing is opaque (Zillow).
3. **Undisclosed referral fees.** The Consumer Policy Center (2026) calls for mandatory up-front disclosure and says 30–40% fees reduce agents' willingness to negotiate. NAR's delegates rejected a disclosure rule (Nov 2025). eXp and Benchmark adopted disclosure forms voluntarily. https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/02/24/could-referral-fees-be-real-estates-next-legal-flashpoint
4. **Mortgage steering and RESPA exposure** (Zillow, Rocket/CFPB).
5. **Non-response:** Zillow's own 2017 data showed 49% of consumers never heard back.
6. **Agent quality variance:** RamseyTrusted mismatches, Clever's first-match misses, rude Movoto agents.

## 3. Post-NAR-settlement landscape (Aug 17, 2024 onward)
- Nationally, buyers must sign a written agreement before touring with an MLS participant. Offers of buyer-agent compensation are banned from the MLS, but sellers can still pay concessions off-MLS.
- **Texas SB 1968 (effective Jan 1, 2026):** any brokerage act for a residential buyer needs a **written agreement**. A showing-only agreement is allowed if it is non-exclusive and lasts 14 days or less. The IABS form was revised for 2026. https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB01968H.htm · https://www.texasrealestate.com/wp-content/uploads/AgencyLawExplainer.pdf · https://neuhausre.com/texas-iabs-form-2026-changes/
- **Confusion persists, and commissions barely moved.** CFA and the National Urban League (April 2026, 223 housing counselors) found "confusion about new commission rules persists." A Consumer Policy Center mystery shop (281 buyer agents, 2025) found 95% ask for 2.5–3% and "little to no uniformity" in buyer agreements. Clever (2024) found 42% of sellers didn't know they were expected to pay the buyer agent. https://consumerfed.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Escalated-Housing-Costs-4.17-.pdf · https://www.rismedia.com/2025/12/04/seller-negotiations-and-3-risk-premiums-what-a-mystery-shopper-study-reveals-about-real-estate-commissions/
- **Opportunity:** none of the competitors explains, before the match, what the buyer agreement will say, what the agent's fee is, and who is likely to pay it. A plain-language "agreement preview" and fee calculator is open ground.

## 4. Texas TREC requirements for our site
- **IABS link (Rule 531.20(b)):** on the **homepage**, readily noticeable, labeled "Texas Real Estate Commission Information About Brokerage Services" (≥10 pt) or "TREC Information About Brokerage Services" (≥12 pt). It must link to our **completed** IABS. Texas Realtors reported a TREC proposal to bar footer-only placement; adoption status is **[UNVERIFIED]**, so put the link in a visible homepage spot, not only the footer. An email-signature link does not count as delivery. https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/consumer-protection-notice
- **Consumer Protection Notice (Rule 531.18):** homepage link labeled "Texas Real Estate Commission Consumer Protection Notice" (≥10 pt) or "TREC Consumer Protection Notice" (≥12 pt), form CN 1-5. https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-531-18
- **Broker name (535.155(a)):** every ad (web, social, email, texts) must show the license holder or team name. When an agent or team is named, the **broker's name must be at least half the size of the largest contact info**. On social media, this information can sit on the profile page if a direct link is provided. Assumed names and team names must be registered with TREC; team names must end in "Team" or "Group."
- **Superlatives and rankings, 535.155(d)(19):** an ad is misleading if it "**ranks the license holder or another service provider unless the ranking is based on objective criteria disclosed in the advertisement**." This bears directly on us: "Top 1% agents," "#1 agent in Austin" or "best agents" require **disclosed objective criteria** next to the claim. Also (d)(1) bars any material inaccuracy, and (d)(12) bars implying involvement in a transaction we were not part of (no borrowing partner agents' "sold" listings as ours without clarity). https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-535-155
- **Promoting other service providers, (d)(18):** recommending partner agents, lenders or title companies for compensation requires **disclosing that we may receive compensation**. RESPA §8 also applies to any lender, title or insurance tie-ins (broker-to-broker referral fees are exempt).
- **Rebates:** allowed in Texas to the party we represent (Rule 535.147). Rebate ads must disclose any consent or conditions. No cash or value over $50 for referrals from unlicensed people (Rule 535.20). Fees can be split only with licensed TX brokers or brokers licensed in another state, so **every out-of-state partner must be a licensed broker or paid through their broker**. https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/tips-rebates-referrals%C2%A0and-other-promotions%C2%A0
- **Other states:** rebates are banned or limited in about 9–10 states (Clever and Rocket list AK, AL, IA, KS, LA, MS, MO, OK, OR, TN; verify current list). Our consumer promises must be state-aware.
- **TCPA:** the FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated (11th Cir., Jan 24 2025), so "consent to be called by marketing partners" forms are still legal. That is exactly why competitors spray leads. A voluntary one-to-one consent policy would differentiate us. https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule

## 6. Gaps we can own
1. **Lowest, disclosed referral fee.** Publish "our partner agents pay us 25% of their commission at closing; you pay $0" on every match screen. That beats HomeLight (33%), Rocket/Movoto (35%) and Zillow (up to 40%), and it gets ahead of the CPC disclosure push and the Zillow class actions. Evidence: sections 1 and 2.3.
2. **One agent, or the 2–3 you choose; never resold.** No "marketing partners" consent box, a voluntary one-to-one TCPA consent, and a written promise that your info goes only to the agents you approve. This answers HomeLight and Realtor.com call-flood complaints directly.
3. **Ranked by verified outcomes, not payment, with criteria shown.** Score agents on verified closed sales (MLS/public records), list-to-sale ratio, days on market, buyer savings, and post-close survey, and **publish the formula**. This is both a differentiator and a TREC 535.155(d)(19) requirement. No placement fees and no premium tiers (unlike FastExpert, Realtor.com and Zillow's invitation routing).
4. **A human broker concierge with a response SLA.** A licensed Texas broker team calls within X minutes in business hours (Clever does about 5) and does a 15-minute needs interview before any agent sees your info. Publish the median response time. This counters Zillow's 49% non-response.
5. **Commission transparency and negotiation help.** Show each agent's typical listing and buyer fee in advance. Include a calculator for "who pays what" after the NAR settlement. Because our partners keep 75%, we can ask them to show fee flexibility. CPC data (95% quote 2.5–3%) shows consumers need this.
6. **A plain-language buyer agreement explainer for Texas SB 1968.** Show sample showing-only (non-exclusive, 14 days or less) and full representation agreements before matching, so buyers are never surprised. CFA found confusion persists, and no competitor does this.
7. **No mortgage or title steering.** A written pledge that agents are never required or paid to push a lender, plus a "compare 3 lenders" pointer. This contrasts with the Zillow Home Loans and Rocket CFPB/RESPA allegations, and TREC Rule 531.1 already pushes this direction.
8. **Spanish-first, not translated-later.** A full Spanish funnel, Spanish-speaking concierge, and filters for agents who speak Spanish and are verified bilingual. Hispanic buyers are the entire growth segment (NAHREP).
9. **New-construction buyer representation.** A "Before you visit a model home" flow that matches buyers with an agent who registers them with the builder first. Builder-paid compensation is explained. This fits Texas's new-build markets; competitors are generic.
10. **A post-close accountability loop.** Every client gets an independent survey we run (not agent-solicited, unlike FastExpert). Agents below threshold are removed, and the removal policy and stats are published. Clever and Ramsey claim removal but publish nothing.
11. **A "switch agents anytime" guarantee.** One click gets a replacement, with no fee and no pressure, plus a clear statement that the buyer agreement terms we promote allow ending early (state-aware). This answers Ramsey and Clever "bad first match" complaints.
12. **Optional state-legal rebate or closing-credit offers, disclosed honestly.** Only where legal and with seller or lender consent language per TREC 535.147. Do not lead with it (Clever's $250 is small), but show the line item if a partner offers it. **[Decision for owner: whether to require partner rebates; check partner-state law]**

Not verified: RamseyTrusted's fee %, FastExpert's fee %, current Rocket Homes cash back, UpNest's 2026 rebrand status, adoption of the TREC IABS footer rule, and the Realtor.com "2–4 agents per lead" figure.
